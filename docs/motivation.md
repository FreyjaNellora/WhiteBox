# Why local sovereignty over the mind's data

WhiteBox begins from **mnemonic sovereignty** — the principle that a person should own, read, and
control the persistent memory an AI holds of them (see the mnemonic-sovereignty framing cited in
[spec/WHITEBOX_v1.1.md](../spec/WHITEBOX_v1.1.md)). But the trajectory of consumer technology
extends that concern from memory to the nervous system itself.

Consumer EEG and other biosignal-sensing devices are moving into everyday products — headphones,
wearables, focus and sleep aids — and an emerging **neuromarketing** industry treats neural and
affective signals as a data source to be harvested and monetized. Neural and affective data are
uniquely sensitive: they are involuntary, hard to falsify, and reveal states a person may not have
chosen to disclose. The **neurorights** movement argues that these signals deserve protection at
the level of a fundamental right.

WhiteBox's stance is that the correct home for such data — if it is ever collected at all — is the
individual's own hardware, under the individual's own control, never a remote service.

**This is a motivation and research direction, not a shipped capability.** WhiteBox does not
process EEG or affective signals today. It is stated here because it explains *why* the security
bar for this project is set where it is: if a system may one day steward the most intimate class
of human data, its defenses must be proven against adversaries *before* that data ever exists —
which is precisely why we adversarially verify the substrate now.
